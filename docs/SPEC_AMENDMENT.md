# SPEC v4.3 Amendment — Planner Explainability, Operational Clarity & Execution Safeguards

**Purpose:** This amendment supplements `SPEC_FINAL_v4_3.md` (the current technical baseline). It makes a small number of operational requirements explicit without changing the frozen data, forecasting results, model choices, scheduling conventions, architecture, or phase sequence.

**Authority:** Read this amendment together with v4.3. If a requirement below appears to conflict with v4.3, stop and report the conflict; do not silently choose or change a frozen decision. Once reviewed and accepted, merge these clarifications into the single authoritative `SPEC.md` and keep this amendment only as change history (or move it to `docs/archive/`). Do not maintain two competing active specifications.

---

## 1. Explain every recommended order

Every proposed supplier order must have an inspectable explanation generated from the deterministic planning engine. The explanation must use the actual inputs and intermediate values used for that schedule line—not a free-form explanation invented by the assistant.

At minimum, the order detail must show:

- Forecast demand over the relevant lead-time/planning window.
- Opening usable stock and how it was derived (on-hand less reserved and blocked stock).
- Expected receipts/open purchase orders counted in the calculation.
- Existing backlog, where applicable.
- Safety stock and reorder point.
- Net requirement before MOQ, order-multiple, rejection gross-up or capacity adjustments.
- Proposed order quantity and a clear breakdown of each adjustment.
- Supplier selected, supplier eligibility, risk factors and SOB target versus realized allocation.
- Supplier capacity available and capacity consumed by this allocation.
- Planning lead time, release week/date and expected arrival week/date.
- Frozen-window status and any warning or failed constraint.
- Final status: `firm`, `unresolved` or `not_ordered`, with a plain-language reason.

The explanation should be available from the schedule table (for example, an expandable row or order-detail panel) and in a downloadable supplier release report. Do not overload the main schedule table with every calculation field.

**Supplier release report/export:** Provide a CSV export for planner review containing, at minimum, run ID, part ID/name, supplier, order quantity, SOB target, realized allocation, MOQ, order multiple, capacity allocation/remaining capacity, risk flags, release date/week, expected arrival date/week, status and unresolved reason. Label it clearly as a *simulated procurement plan—not a purchase order or supplier commitment*.

## 2. Critical spare-part priority must be visible

The existing scheduling priority rules remain authoritative. This amendment makes their presentation and validation explicit:

- Critical-part shortages and late arrivals must appear before ordinary inventory/excess-stock alerts on the overview.
- Show the affected part, criticality, first shortage week, shortage/backlog quantity, expected replenishment week, supplier/capacity issue and evidence behind the warning.
- A critical part that cannot be supplied in time must remain visibly `unresolved`; never hide the exposure by changing the forecast, suppressing the shortage, or inventing emergency supply.
- The user must be able to filter the schedule and warnings to critical parts.
- Tests must verify that critical backlog is allocated before current demand in accordance with the existing simulator contract.

Do not introduce a new optimization objective or change the frozen scheduler weights through this amendment.

## 3. Supplier risk must be explainable

A supplier risk flag or score must be accompanied by its available underlying evidence. Display the factors that are actually present in the supplied data or generated by the documented simulator, such as:

- On-time delivery / late-delivery share.
- Average delivery delay and the resulting planning lead-time adjustment.
- Rejection share and the resulting gross-up to order quantity.
- Available monthly capacity after committed capacity.
- Reliability information, where supplied, with a clear note that reliability must not be double-counted with OTD and rejection.
- Whether the risk value is measured from supplied data, simulated, or an assumption/proxy.

For each supplier allocation, show which factor affected eligibility, lead time, gross-up, capacity or warning status. If a factor is unavailable, say `not available`; do not infer or fabricate it. Keep the existing v4.3 risk formulas and thresholds unless a separately approved, evidence-backed change is recorded.

## 4. KPI presentation and claims

The ten mentor KPIs and their v4.3 formulas remain unchanged. For every KPI, the UI and report must make the evidence basis unmistakable:

- `measured`: directly calculated from supplied data.
- `simulated`: produced by the documented inventory/scheduling simulation.
- `proxy`: depends on an explicit assumption or estimated cost.
- `not_measurable`: the available data cannot support a defensible value.

Show the formula, unit, baseline, proposed value, denominator/sample count where relevant, and the basis label. State the assumptions beside proxy-based cost results. In particular:

- Carrying cost may be estimated only using the configured data rate or explicitly labelled fallback assumption.
- Emergency procurement cost/reduction is simulated or proxy-based according to its inputs.
- Actual production downtime cost or actual business savings must not be claimed without company evidence.
- Inventory turnover must remain unavailable unless the required demand/COGS and average inventory-value data are present.
- If a baseline is zero, show an absolute difference or `not defined`, never a percentage reduction.
- Show negative outcomes and KPIs where the proposed policy performs worse than baseline; do not selectively present improvements.

Use the statement **“Method validated on simulated data”** wherever results might otherwise be mistaken for real-world validation. Never describe simulated results as realized savings or supplier commitments.

## 5. Forecast horizon and uncertainty display

Retain the validated 14-week weekly forecast horizon. Wherever the forecast is displayed or exported, show the exact first and last forecast dates and the number of weeks. Describe it as approximately three months, not exactly three calendar months.

Show the point forecast and the available uncertainty information (quantiles or a clearly labelled interval) without crowding the main view. Make short-history/analogue forecasts and their low-confidence status visually distinct. Do not imply that a forecast interval is a probability of the event unless its calibration supports that interpretation.

## 6. Data import: simple and validated

The supplied CSVs remain the initial source of truth. If a user-facing import feature is implemented, keep it limited to an authorized admin workflow:

1. Select the expected dataset type.
2. Preview detected columns, row count, date range and key fields.
3. Validate schema, types, required fields, duplicate keys, missing-week rules and permitted date/split roles.
4. Show row-level errors and warnings before import.
5. Import only after explicit confirmation; reject invalid files without partially replacing the active dataset.
6. Preserve original uploaded files and record file hash, import time, validation result and dataset version.

Do not build a generic ETL platform or allow imported data to rewrite frozen benchmark files, splits or historical results. If import is outside the MVP time budget, document the supported command-line data-load procedure instead; this item must not block the core forecasting/scheduling workflow.

## 7. Verify frozen Phase 4 evidence; do not rerun it by default

Before proceeding with unfinished implementation work, perform a read-only evidence audit of the Phase 4 champion stated in v4.3:

- Confirm the recorded champion, score, 14/14 sensitivity result, candidate eligibility and overlap-removed scenario against the existing selection matrix, configuration, result files and `STATE.md`.
- Confirm the sealed final test remains untouched.
- Report missing or contradictory evidence explicitly.
- Do not retrain, retune, rerun the selection process, change weights, replace the champion or open the sealed test merely to complete this audit.
- If the evidence cannot be found or does not support the recorded claim, stop and ask for a decision. Do not silently rewrite the v4.3 result.

This is a provenance check, not a new model-selection phase.

## 8. Testing and implementation order

The v4.3 phase order and release-blocking gates remain binding. The following order is emphasized:

1. Complete the deterministic calculation engine and its hand-calculated tests.
2. Pass the independent schedule checker and inventory-conservation/determinism tests.
3. Verify supplier allocation, capacity, lead-time and unresolved outcomes.
4. Pass API authorization, proposal lifecycle, prompt-injection and audit tests.
5. Only then complete dashboard polish, exports and presentation refinements.

A dashboard, chart, assistant response or demo script must never conceal a failed calculation or test. Do not mark a phase complete until its specified gate passes on the required environments. Do not weaken tests, acceptance criteria or constraints to make the demonstration look successful.

## 9. Optional SLM boundary

The v4.3 scope remains unchanged: rules/classifier fallback and a structured-output multilingual LLM are sufficient for the MVP. An industry-specific SLM is optional post-MVP experimentation only. Do not train a model from scratch, make SLM availability a project dependency, or delay the tested MVP for it.
